How this works

DOCX → PDF

The file is rendered in-browser by docx-preview, a proper OOXML renderer that parses document.xml, styles.xml, theme1.xml, and the header/footer parts to produce a layout that matches what Word renders.

The rendered page sections are then handed to html2canvas and jsPDF, which compose the PDF programmatically — without going through the browser's print engine. This eliminates the URL, date, and page-number captions that browsers inject automatically, and reclaims the ~30 mm of vertical space they consume.

PDF → DOCX

The file is parsed by PDF.js, which returns positioned text fragments per page. Fragments are grouped into lines by Y-coordinate, lines into paragraphs by vertical gap, and each paragraph is classified as heading or body by comparing its font size to the document's median.

The result is assembled into document.xml, packaged with the minimum OOXML scaffolding, and zipped into a real .docx you can open in Word. Best for prose-heavy documents — diagrams and multi-column layouts may not survive the round-trip.
